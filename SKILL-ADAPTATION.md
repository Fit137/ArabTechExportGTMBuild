The skill is `B2B SaaS Go-To-Market Builder`. Arab Tech Export sells services and education to two audiences. Six of the nine phases assume software and need a substitution, and three run unchanged.

Read this before Phase 1. Without it the run produces per-seat SaaS pricing tiers and a G2 review analysis for a business that has neither.

## Phase 1. Product Feature Matrix becomes the Service and Offer Matrix

A service business cannot be scored on demand and differentiation alone. A well-differentiated, in-demand service can still be wrong to keep if it costs too much to deliver or does the wrong job in the funnel.

Rate every item in `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 3 on six vectors instead of two:

| # | Vector | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Client Demand | How strongly the market pulls for this service |
| 2 | Competitive Differentiation | How distinguishable it is from what others offer |
| 3 | Delivery Effort | What it costs to fulfil |
| 4 | Sales Motion | How it has to be sold |
| 5 | Ticket Size | Where it sits on price |
| 6 | Funnel Role | What job it does, entry point through anchor |

**Keep the skill's mechanics.** Steps 1.3 through 1.5 plot two axes and assign quadrants. Plot Client Demand against Competitive Differentiation exactly as written, decimals and all, and carry vectors 3 to 6 as a second table read alongside the scatter. Steps 1.6 and 1.7, the demand driver, run unchanged and feed both the lead magnet and the top of funnel asset in Phase 8.

**Watch for this result.** One-on-ones will almost certainly score high on demand and differentiation and terribly on delivery effort, because they consume founder hours that do not scale. That is the finding, and the two-vector version would have hidden it.

## Phase 2. Competitive analysis, with different sources

Steps 2.1 and 2.7 tell you to crawl G2, Capterra, and Trustpilot. Those carry software reviews. This category is not reviewed there and the searches come back empty or, worse, return an unrelated software company.

Substitute sources, in order of usefulness:

1. Public discussion where the audience actually talks. Reddit threads, X threads, YouTube comments under the competitor's own content, and Discord or Slack communities.
2. Course and community platform reviews, wherever the competitor sells.
3. The competitor's own refund policy, terms, and public testimonials. What they promise and how they hedge it tells you what they get challenged on.
4. Trustpilot, which does carry some training and education businesses.
5. For Track A slot 4, the hire, there is no website. Profile it from job postings for target-market sales leaders: compensation band, required experience, and time to hire. That is its cost and its weakness.

**Step 2.7 substitution.** The skill wants a four and five star versus one to three star breakdown. Ask the same question of the substitute sources: what do satisfied members say they got, and what do the unsatisfied ones say they did not. For a community the recurring complaint is almost always that the room went quiet or the promised access was not there. Bring that finding into Phase 7 stage 5, because it is the same failure the Arab Tech Export community will face.

## Phase 3. Positioning, unchanged

The three statements land as written. Note what the status quo statement is for each track, since it is the one most people pick:

- **Track B status quo:** keep learning from free content and keep bidding on marketplaces.
- **Track A status quo:** keep selling at home and revisit the target market later.

## Phase 4. ICP, reinterpret the two levels

The skill's levels are "MVP to PMF" and "Growth". Those are software company stages. Read them as buyer maturity against their own commercial motion.

| | ICP Level 1 | ICP Level 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Track B** | Can build, has no target-market revenue yet. Selling locally or on marketplaces | Has some target-market revenue and is trying to raise price, repeat it, or make it consistent |
| **Track A** | Established at home, no deliberate target-market motion | Early target-market traction, selling there without a strategy or a trained team |

**Two fields need changing for Track B.** "Revenue, $" becomes the individual's current income band from their own building, not a company revenue. "Team Size" becomes solo, or solo with contractors. Leave "Business Model" as how they currently charge, which is the interesting field for this audience: hourly, per project, retainer, or product.

## Phase 5. Buying committee

**Track A runs as written.** The three levels are real: User is the sales rep being trained, Manager is the VP Sales or Sales Director, Decision Maker is the founder or CEO. Fill the "Potential Reasons to Block" row for the Manager carefully. A training engagement bought over a sales leader's head, framed as fixing their team, dies quietly in scheduling regardless of content quality.

**Track B has a committee of one.** Do not invent three personas to fill the table. Fill one column honestly and note the collapse.

Then put the effort into Step 5.2, the Design Partner profile. For a pre-launch community that step is the most valuable in the phase, because it defines the founding cohort, and a founding cohort is the only thing that makes stage 5 of the funnel work in month one. Treat 5.2 as a Track B deliverable in its own right rather than an appendix to 5.1.

## Phase 6. Value proposition, with one blocked field

Runs as written. Three pillars, nine reasons to believe, nine copy drills.

**Two fields will be empty and must stay empty.** Every copy drill has a "Supporting Proof Point" line, and one of its three headline types is "Headline (Social Proof)". Arab Tech Export has no proof and no social proof. Nine copy drills times two fields is eighteen invitations to invent a customer.

Fill both with `{TBD}` unless the founder's own background, open decision 5 in the brief, supplies something real. Method and process are legitimate substitutes for the social proof headline: what the approach is and what it demands of the buyer signals seriousness without claiming a result.

This is the phase where a fabricated testimonial is most likely to enter the run, because the template asks for one nine times.

## Phase 7. Customer journey, unchanged

Seven stages work for both tracks. Two notes:

- **Track B stage 5, Value Realization**, is the whole product. Content converts people into stage 4 and does not hold them past it. Whatever the substitute review research in Phase 2 surfaced about communities going quiet belongs in the Primary Journey Blockers list.
- **Track A stage 7, Renewal and Loyalty**, reads as a follow-on engagement rather than a subscription renewal.

Add one item to the Primary Journey Blockers for both tracks: whether the buyer can actually pay. Payment rails and cross-border card availability vary across the origin markets, and a checkout the buyer cannot complete converts at zero. That is an infrastructure blocker sitting inside a marketing funnel, and the funnel map is where it becomes visible.

## Phase 8. Assets library, substitute the channels

The skill hardcodes three campaigns: LinkedIn Outreach via HeyReach, Cold Email via Instantly, and LinkedIn Ads. Keep the three-channel structure and the top, middle, and bottom of funnel breakdown. Change which three.

**Track B.** Cold email and paid ads to individuals are the wrong instruments here, and a business teaching market access that cold-emails people is a bad look. Paid ads against an unproven offer with no conversion data buys expensive evidence. Use instead:

1. Founder-led organic, short form
2. Long-form video or writing, the depth asset that separates this from free content
3. Email, the only channel not rented from a platform

**Track A.** Closer to the skill's defaults. Use:

1. Founder-led professional network, organic
2. Partnerships with trade bodies, accelerators, and export programs
3. Direct outreach, which is defensible for enterprise

Partnerships deserve the most attention. Track A's binding constraint is having no track record, and a credible institutional partner substitutes for one in a way that no content asset can.

## Phase 9. Pricing

**Track B runs as written.** Decoy, Hero, Anchor fits a membership. Two changes to the value metric table: per-seat and per-user are the same thing for a solo member, so score per-member, per-cohort, and per-access-tier instead. Drop the outcome denominator, because pricing a training product on the member's earned outcome is an income claim with a payment schedule attached.

Then hold the affordability tension explicitly: a target-market price is a significant amount of money at a local income. Regional pricing is a real decision with real leakage risks, and it belongs in this phase rather than being discovered at launch.

**Track A does not use tiers.** Four subscription plans do not describe a consulting engagement. Replace Step 9.2 with engagement pricing: scope, duration, and what a second engagement costs.

Keep Step 9.3 ROI anchoring for Track A, because it is the strongest artifact in the phase for this buyer. Anchor 2, the alternative comparison, is the one that closes: set the engagement against the fully loaded cost of hiring a target-market sales leader, against an agency's margin, and against the cost of one failed market entry attempt.

**If pricing is deferred**, open decision 3 in the brief, every price is `{TBD}` and the phase still runs. The tier shape, the value metric, and the ROI structure are worth having before the number exists.

## Lead Magnet scorecard, strong fit

Runs as written and it is the single deliverable in the run that does the most work for Track B, because it converts the thesis into something a stranger can experience in two minutes.

The scorecard should reveal the access gap rather than test knowledge. A builder scoring low is not being told they are unskilled, which would be both wrong and insulting. They are being shown which parts of reaching a target-market buyer they have never had to handle. That distinction is the whole design, and it is the same distinction the claim constraints in the brief are protecting.

## The 10-slide deck, unchanged

Runs last. It reads from thirteen inputs produced by the earlier phases, so it cannot run early.

Its own prompt already carries two rules this project needs: never use the em dash, and keep unknown metrics as `{TBD}` without inventing numbers. Both apply to the entire run, not just the deck.
