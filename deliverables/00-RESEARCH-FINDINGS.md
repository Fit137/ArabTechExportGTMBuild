# External research findings

Covers the three research steps the skill hands to the user, plus the Step 0 the
repo's `RESEARCH-PROMPTS.md` adds. Executed 2026-09-12 by web search and page
fetch from inside the Claude Code session.

Every row below is either a named entity with a working URL or is marked as not
found. Nothing here is inferred. Where the research came back thin, the row says
thin rather than filling the gap, per `RESEARCH-PROMPTS.md`.

**Confidence key**

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **V** | Verified. Named entity, working URL, corroborated by at least two independent results |
| **P** | Partial. Entity confirmed, but pricing, scope, or regional focus not confirmed from a primary page |
| **T** | Thin. The research direction returned no verifiable named entity. Recorded as thin |

One access limit to note: `arabtechexport.com` could not be fetched from this
session, the egress proxy blocks the domain. The Arab Tech Export row in Phase 2

**Slot 2 finding, and it matters.** The research looked for one large named
Arabic creator teaching this audience to sell to Western clients and did not find
one. What it found instead is a diffuse supply: Hsoub Academy's free library of
more than 3,500 articles and 11 books, long Arabic YouTube playlists explaining
Upwork end to end, individual videos with high view counts, and the Freelancers
Arab app aggregating platform guidance.

Read that as a strategic fact rather than a gap in the research. The free
competition in this niche is fragmented, not concentrated. Nobody owns the
category's attention. That is the opening, and it changes Phase 8: an organic
content channel is competing against volume, not against a brand.

Before Phase 2 is final, name the one or two creators your own audience actually
watches. That is a question about your audience, not a question the open web can
answer, and it is the single highest-value 20 minutes in this whole run.

**Slot 1 finding, and it is the most commercially important line in this
document.** The search for a named consultancy that takes Arab technology
companies into the United States and Canada returned consultancies going the
other way. AstroLabs takes international companies into the Gulf. YCP advises on
Middle East go to market. The Big Four and MBB firms are staffed in the region to
serve regional and inbound work.

The outbound lane is thinly served by named specialists. That is what
`GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5 was protecting when it banned the superlative claim, and
the research supports the narrowed form: describe what the business is built
around, a specific origin and a specific destination. It does not support "the
only provider". Absence of evidence from one research pass is not proof of
absence, and a boutique with no search footprint is exactly the kind of
competitor this method misses.

## Step 1.1. Service demand research

Against the eight Track B service items in `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 3.

| # | Service item | Anyone in the set already offering it | Evidence buyers want it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | School community access, the peer room | Yes, widely. The Futur Pro Group, most Skool and Circle communities | Strong, and strongly conditional. Value tracks activity, see Step 2.7 |
| 2 | Structured courses | Yes, saturated. Hsoub Academy, The Futur, Udemy, Egypt FWD free | Strong for the subject, weak as a differentiator. Supply is enormous and much of it is free |
| 3 | One on one sessions | Rare at low price points, common as a premium upsell | Strong. Access to the founder is a recurring theme in what buyers say they bought |
| 4 | AMA sessions | Common in paid communities | Moderate. Rated highly when attended, attendance decays |
| 5 | Pricing and proposal teardowns on a live deal | The Futur does this publicly on creative work. Not found for AI services into North America | Inferred strong, not verified. The nearest verified analogue is The Futur's public critique format |
| 6 | Positioning review for a target market buyer | Not found as a named, packaged offer in this niche | `{TBD}` |
| 7 | Peer deal review against a rubric | Not found as a named, packaged offer | `{TBD}` |
| 8 | Buyer evaluation walkthroughs | Not found as a named, packaged offer | `{TBD}` |

**The shape of this table is the finding.** Items 1 to 4, the stated offer, are
the ones the competitor set already covers. Items 5 to 8, the candidates, are the
ones nobody in the verified set packages. That is exactly the split Phase 1 is
built to find, and it points the differentiation at the work done on the
member's own live deal rather than at the curriculum.

The `{TBD}` marks on 6, 7 and 8 are honest. No published evidence of demand was
found, which is different from evidence of no demand, and the correct next step is
the design partner conversation in Phase 5 Step 5.2, not a guess here.

## Step 2.7 substitute. Buyer feedback themes

The skill sends you to G2. This category is not on G2. Substituted sources per
`SKILL-ADAPTATION.md` Phase 2.

### What buyers praise

| Theme | Where it appears | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Access to the person, not the content | The Futur, paid communities generally | High |
| Accountability and seeing peers act in real time | Cohort based freelancing programs | High |
| Practical application over theory | Hsoub Academy's stated model, assessed by exam and interview | High |
| Price and accessibility in Arabic | Hsoub Academy, Udemy Arabic courses, Egypt FWD at zero cost | High, and it sets the price ceiling |

### What buyers complain about

| Theme | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| The room goes quiet | Churn in the first 14 days runs 30 to 50 percent on cohorts that were not onboarded well. Monthly churn in the 8 to 10 percent range is read as an onboarding failure rather than a pricing one | **High, quantified** |
| Never found the right starting point, never posted, cancelled before the second bill | Same source, described as the dominant early churn pattern | High |
| Refund terms are at the creator's discretion, with no standard policy on community platforms | Platform documentation | Moderate |
| Complaints concentrate on specific paid groups rather than on platforms | Public discussion | Moderate |

**This is the most important finding in the run.** `SKILL-ADAPTATION.md`
predicted the complaint before the research ran, and the research put a number on
it. A paid community's failure mode is not bad content. It is a first 14 days in
which the member does not act, does not post, and is not seen.

Carried into Phase 7 stage 5 as the primary blocker, into Phase 9 as what a member
is actually paying to avoid, and into Phase 5 Step 5.2 as the reason the founding
cohort has to be recruited rather than accepted.

## Hard commercial constraint. Payment rails

`SKILL-ADAPTATION.md` Phase 7 asks for one added blocker, whether the buyer can
actually pay. The research says this is not a footnote for Track B, it is a
packaging constraint that reaches Phase 9.

| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| Egyptian card caps | An EGP card paying a USD charge triggers a cross border conversion that counts against the card's international spending limit. Monthly USD caps are reported as low as $50 to $200. Past the cap, charges decline regardless of the balance in the account |
| Egyptian debit cards | International limits reported as currently suspended on all debit cards |
| Illustrative case | A $20 monthly charge can clear once and then fail at renewal when the limit is reached |
| Foreign exchange fees | NBE and CIB cut card FX fees from 5 percent to 3 percent. Banque Misr announced the same, effective 13 August 2025 |
| Wise | Cards not available in Egypt. Residents cannot send from EGP. Accounts can receive and hold |
| Payoneer | Supported for platform payouts across Upwork, Fiverr, Toptal. The workhorse for inbound freelance income |
| Morocco | Cash Plus and PayPal partnership announced September 2025, enabling withdrawal of PayPal balances in dirhams |

**What this does to the run.** A monthly USD subscription is not reliably
chargeable to a member in the largest origin market by developer population. The
payment method is not a checkout detail to be sorted at launch, it is an input to
the tier design in Phase 9. Annual and cohort billing, local currency collection,
and a non card route all move from nice to necessary. This is carried into Phase 7
stage 4, Phase 9 Step 9.2, and Phase 8 as a bottom of funnel asset.
