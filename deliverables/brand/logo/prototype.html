Arab Tech Export, logo prototypes

Three directions, all built from Islamic architecture rather than Islamic starwork. No star form appears in any of them. Green throughout. Upload this file to Claude Design with the accompanying prompt.

What was wrong with the first attempt

Two squares at 0 and 45 degrees produce an eight point star. That is authentic Islamic geometry, and the argument is worthless, because a mark that needs explaining has already failed. In flat blue, angular, centred, it reads the way it reads. Star forms are now banned outright, alongside camels, lamps, domes and calligraphy as ornament. Blue is banned too.

Direction 1 · The Gate · recommended

Full mark
four segments, four thresholds
Reduced
under 24px, embroidery, engraving
On the deep band
One colour

The idea. A pointed arch, open at the base. The band is split into four segments, which are the four departments that decide and the four bands of the ladder. It is a gate you pass through, not a badge you wear. Architecture, not ornament, and it reads at any size. Under 24px the segmentation drops and it becomes one clean arch, which is a legitimate responsive reduction rather than a different mark.

Direction 2 · The Arcade

Three arches, one route
On the deep band
One colour

The idea. A riwaq, the arcaded walk of a caravanserai, the roadside inn that trade routes were built around. Three arches receding, brightening toward the destination. Trade, passage and hospitality in one form. Caveat: it must mirror under RTL, and the opacity ramp is fragile in one colour and on embroidery.

Direction 3 · The Weave

Two bands, interlaced
On the deep band

The idea. Girih strapwork without a star. Two bands pass over and under each other, origin and destination locked together. Caveat, and it is the reason this is third: a rotated square plus a square is one step away from the shape we are trying to avoid. It is safer than an eight point star and it is not safe enough. Included so the comparison is on the table, not because I would ship it.
