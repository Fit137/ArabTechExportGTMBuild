## Step 1.4. Quadrant assignment

| Service item | Quadrant | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer evaluation walkthroughs | 1. Hero Feature | High demand and nobody in the verified set packages it. Shows what a North American buyer actually checks, which is the one thing free content cannot demonstrate because free content is made by people selling to a different audience |
| Pricing and proposal teardowns on a live deal | 1. Hero Feature | Slightly lower demand because the member has to have a live deal to bring. The highest differentiation score in the set. The Futur does public critique on creative work, nobody found doing it on AI services into North America |
| Positioning review for a target market buyer | 2. Differentiator | Real differentiation, demand is narrower because members do not know they need it until they lose a deal |
| One on one sessions | 2. Differentiator | Access to the founder is the most praised thing in the whole competitor set. Demand is capped because price puts it out of reach for many, not because it is unwanted |
| Peer deal review against a rubric | 2. Differentiator | The only negative demand score. Members want the founder's eyes, not a peer's. Keep it as community mechanics, do not sell it as a feature |
| School community access | 4. Table Stakes | High demand, no differentiation. Every competitor has a room. Having a room is not an offer, see the delivery note below |
| AMA sessions | 3. Commodity | Low on both. Rated well when attended, attendance decays. Runs as retention mechanics, not as a reason to buy |
| Structured courses | 4. Table Stakes | Strong demand, negative differentiation. The category's free supply sets the ceiling here and it sits at zero |

**Lead with quadrant 1.** Buyer evaluation walkthroughs and live deal teardowns.
Both are about the member's actual deal against an actual target market buyer, not
about curriculum. That is the line the whole GTM should run along.

## Step 1.5. Entry validation

| Rule | Check | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Wanted by some excludes Most vendors can do this | Item 4, AMA sessions, is Wanted by some and Most vendors can do this | **Violation** |
| Wanted by all excludes Unique to us | No item scored Wanted by all | Pass |
| Wanted by one excludes Commodity should be free | Item 7 is Wanted by one and A few others can do this | Pass |

**Resolution for item 4.** AMA sessions were rated by the format rather than by
what the format delivers. The demand is for founder access, which is item 3, and
an AMA is a cheaper delivery of it. Corrected to **Wanted by most, Commodity
should be free**, which moves it to quadrant 4 and passes the rules. Scores
adjusted to demand 8.1, competitiveness minus 11.2.

That correction is not bookkeeping. It says AMAs are a cost saving on one to ones,
not a product, and they should be priced and positioned as such.

## The six vector table

Vectors 3 to 6, read alongside the scatter. This is the half of the phase that the
two vector version would have hidden.

| # | Service item | Delivery effort | Sales motion | Ticket size | Funnel role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | School community access | Low per member, high to keep alive | Self serve | Low, recurring | Retention core |
| 2 | Structured courses | High once, near zero after | Self serve | Low to mid | Entry point |
| 3 | One on one sessions | **Very high, founder hours, does not scale** | Assisted | High | Anchor |
| 4 | AMA sessions | Medium, founder hours but one to many | Self serve | Bundled | Retention |
| 5 | Live deal teardowns | **High, founder hours, partly scalable one to many** | Assisted | Mid to high | **Differentiator and proof** |
| 6 | Positioning review | Medium to high | Assisted | Mid | Upsell |
| 7 | Peer deal review | Low once the rubric exists | Self serve | Bundled | Retention mechanics |
| 8 | Buyer evaluation walkthroughs | High once, low after | Self serve | Bundled | **Hero, and top of funnel proof** |

### The finding the adaptation predicted

One to ones score 6.9 and 7.1, respectable on both plotted axes, and they are the
single worst item in the set on delivery effort. Founder hours, nothing gained from
repetition, and a hard ceiling set by the number of hours in a week.

The two vector version would have shown a decent differentiator. The six vector
version shows the thing that will cap the business at the founder's calendar if it
sits at the centre of the offer.

**What to do with it.** Do not cut it and do not centre it. Price it as the anchor
tier, cap the number of slots, and use its scarcity to make the tier below it look
correct. The demand for founder access is real and it is the most praised thing in
the competitor set. The answer is to meet that demand one to many wherever
possible, through items 5 and 8, and to keep item 3 as the expensive, limited
version.

### The second finding, which the adaptation did not predict

Items 5 and 8, the two quadrant 1 items, are also the two highest delivery effort
items that are not one to ones. The differentiation and the delivery cost sit in
the same place.

That is not a reason to avoid them. It is a reason to build item 8 first, because
buyer evaluation walkthroughs are high effort once and low effort after, while
teardowns are high effort every time. Build the asset that amortises, then sell the
one that does not.

## Step 1.6. Demand driver

| Attribute | Description |
|---|---|
| What it is | A short self assessment scoring a builder on six evidenced dimensions from `00-BUYER-CHECKS.md`, returning a per dimension breakdown and a band position rather than a single verdict |
| Immediate value | The builder sees the buyer's checklist. Most have never seen one, because the checks happen after the conversation ends and nobody tells you which one you failed |
| How it helps in problem identification | It separates the two reasons a deal is lost. Capability, which this audience mostly has, and legibility to the buyer, which is the thing nobody taught them. Scoring those apart is the insight |
| How it exposes the need for Arab Tech Export | A low score is not solvable by getting better at building, which is what this audience does when a deal is lost. Part of it is solvable by presenting differently and part of it has to be built. Knowing which is which is the offer, per `00-RESOLUTIONS.md` conflict 3 |
| ICP fit | Direct. ICP Level 1 in Phase 4, can build, no target market revenue yet. The band result is what routes Level 1 and Level 2 differently |
| Conversion pull | The score is a stated problem in the builder's own words, captured before any pitch. Every later asset answers the dimension they scored lowest on |
| Top of funnel use case | Primary. Entry to the email sequence and the qualification route into the community |

*Field renamed from the skill's "Leverage for conversion" per the word list in `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5. Same field, same position in the skill's Step 1.6 table.*

**The design rule that governs it.** The scorecard reveals an access gap, never a
skill deficit. A low score says which parts of reaching a target market buyer this
person has never had to handle. It never says they are unskilled, which would be
both wrong and insulting to an audience that can build. See
`GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5 and `SKILL-ADAPTATION.md`, Lead Magnet.

## Step 1.7. Demand driver variants

| Variant | Perceived value | Ease of delivery | Delivery type | Description | TOFU use case |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buyer Readiness Scorecard | High | Easy | Self assessment scorecard, ScoreApp | The six dimension self assessment above. Instant, scales infinitely | **Primary.** Built in full in `10-lead-magnet-scorecard.md` |
| The Buyer's Checklist | Medium | Easy | Static asset | The raw checklist without scoring. Lower value because no personal result, cheapest to make | Lead magnet for a second channel, or the content behind an organic post |
| Proposal teardown, public | High | Medium | Content, one to many | A real anonymised proposal marked up against the checklist, published | Recurring organic series. Doubles as proof of the quadrant 1 service |
| Live proposal review | Very high | Hard | Service, white glove | The member's own proposal, reviewed live | Bottom of funnel only. Founder hours, does not scale, matches the Phase 1 delivery effort warning |
| Rate and scope calibration tool | Medium | Medium | Light tool | Shows how a scope of work is described and priced for a target market buyer against how it is described at home | Secondary. Handle with care, see below |

**A warning on the last one.** A tool that compares pricing gets within one step of
an income comparison, which `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5 bans outright. If it is built,
it compares how work is *described and scoped*, never what anyone earned. If that
line cannot be held in the interface, do not build it.
