## Key journey insights

### Critical success factors

1. **The first 14 days decide the business.** Stage 5's three KPIs are the only ones
   that matter in year one. Everything upstream is worthless if members arrive and
   go quiet.
2. **The scorecard has to qualify, not just capture.** Its job is to send Level 1
   into the email list and Level 2 into the room. A scorecard optimised for
   completions rather than for sorting will fill stage 4 with people who cannot use
   the differentiated half of the offer.
3. **Every member needs a live deal.** VP2 and both quadrant 1 services require one.
   A member without a deal has bought a course, and the course half is the
   commodity half.
4. **The founder has to be present at stage 3 and stage 5.** There is nothing else
   to evaluate and nothing else that makes the room feel real in month one.

### Primary journey blockers

1. **Stage 3 is currently blocked outright.** There is no founder background page
   because `00-ASSUMPTIONS.md` decision 5 is unanswered. A buyer who reaches stage 3
   and finds nothing to evaluate does not proceed. This is the single largest
   structural hole in the Track B funnel and it is fixable in an afternoon.
2. **Stage 4, the buyer may not be able to pay.** Not a metaphor. In Egypt, monthly
   USD card caps are reported as low as $50 to $200 and international limits are
   reported suspended on debit cards. A recurring USD card charge is unreliable in
   one of the largest origin markets by developer population. A checkout the buyer
   cannot complete converts at zero regardless of how good stage 3 was. Track
   payment failure by market from day one, because this failure is invisible in an
   aggregate conversion rate.
3. **Stage 5, the room goes quiet.** Documented at 30 to 50 percent churn in the
   first 14 days for poorly onboarded cohorts, with monthly churn of 8 to 10 percent
   read as an onboarding failure rather than a pricing one. The pattern is
   consistent: joined with high intent, never found the right starting point, never
   posted, cancelled before the second bill. This is the failure mode Arab Tech
   Export will face in month two and it is the same one the competitor research
   found everywhere.
4. **Stage 1 competes against free, and the free is good.** Egypt FWD is free and
   government funded. Hsoub publishes thousands of free articles. Awareness content
   here is not competing against silence, it is competing for attention against
   genuinely useful free material.

### Added from Block 1 research, why buyers actually go quiet

The original version of stage 1 treated the unexplained loss as something the member
never gets a reason for. `00-BUYER-CHECKS.md` supplies the mechanism and it is a
better story than the one this funnel was built on.

**The champion could not sell the vendor internally.** A North American buyer runs
four parallel lanes, procurement, legal and privacy, IT and security, and finance,
and go-live is gated on all four clearing. Deals stall when the buyer-side advocate
has nothing to hand each lane. The good first call was real. What followed was an
advocate carrying no documents into three departments that never met the vendor.

Separately, a 90 to 180 day enterprise cycle with no contact looks identical to
rejection from outside. Some of what members read as ghosting is a normal cycle.

**What this changes in this funnel.** Stage 1's trigger stays the same, an
unexplained loss. What the member is taught to do about it changes from presenting
better to arming the champion. That is a different asset set and a more teachable
one, and it should be reflected here once `00-CONFLICTS-TO-RESOLVE.md` conflict 2
settles who this track is for.

### Acceleration opportunities

1. **Compress stage 2 to stage 5 for the founding cohort.** Eight to twelve people
   recruited directly skip stages 1 through 4 entirely. That is the fastest route to
   a room that is not empty, and Phase 5 Step 5.2 is the mechanism.
2. **Make stage 5's first action a booking, not a browse.** A teardown slot booked in
   week one converts a passive member into someone with a deadline. This is the
   single highest impact change available against the 14 day churn number.
3. **Publish the checklist for free and in full.** It costs one asset and it does
   stage 1 and stage 2 work at the same time. The offer is not the checklist, it is
   the application of it to the member's own deal, which cannot be given away.
4. **Solve stage 4 payment as a growth project, not an operations task.** Annual
   billing, cohort billing, and a local collection route each recover members who
   already decided to buy. That is the cheapest conversion available anywhere in
   this funnel.
