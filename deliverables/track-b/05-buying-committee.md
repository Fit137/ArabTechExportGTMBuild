# Track B, Phase 5. Buying Committee

Step 5.1 collapses to one person per `SKILL-ADAPTATION.md`. Step 5.2 is promoted to

---

## Step 5.1. The committee of one

Track B's buyer is an individual spending their own money on themselves. There is
no user, no manager, and no budget holder. There is one person holding all three
roles at once and in conflict with themselves.

Filling three persona columns here would produce a fiction. One column, filled
honestly, plus the internal conflict that actually decides the purchase.

| Attribute | The individual builder |
|---|---|
| Possible job titles | Software engineer, senior engineer, full stack developer, machine learning engineer, AI engineer, indie hacker, technical founder, freelance developer, AI consultant, automation consultant, solo agency owner |
| Responsibility area | Everything. Builds it, sells it, bills it, supports it |
| Purchasing influence | Total, and unconstrained by anyone else. Also unsupported by anyone else |
| Core journey stages | All seven. The same person is present at every stage, which means there is no internal champion to carry the decision while they are busy |
| **Potential reasons to block** | Price against a free alternative that is genuinely good. Suspicion of the whole online course category, which is crowded with bad actors. No stateable founder credentials yet, see `00-ASSUMPTIONS.md` decision 5. Cannot complete the payment, see the rail constraints. Believes the gap is skill and buys a technical course instead. Has been in a paid community before that went quiet. Language uncertainty about operating in English |
| Drivers | A named loss they cannot explain. Watching someone visibly weaker win the work. Local price ceiling reached. Wanting to be around people doing the same thing. Currency or income instability at home making foreign income feel safer |
| Assets to get buy-in | Buyer Readiness Scorecard with a per dimension result. Published buyer evaluation walkthrough. A real anonymised proposal teardown. Clear refund and cancellation terms, stated up front. An honest page about who this is not for |
| Channels to find them | Arabic organic short form video. Long form video and writing. Email. Arabic developer and freelancer communities. Under the free content they already watch |

### The conflict that actually decides it

The three roles do not disappear when they collapse into one person, they argue.

| Role | What it says |
|---|---|
| The user | "I want to know what the buyer checks. This is the only place I have seen that."  |
| The manager | "I have bought a course before and did not finish it. What makes this different." |
| The decision maker | "This is real money at my income, against free alternatives that are good." |

The user is sold before the page loads. The manager and the decision maker are not,
and no amount of value proposition copy answers them. The manager is answered by
mechanics, a structure that makes not finishing visible and uncomfortable. The
decision maker is answered by price, terms, and a working payment route.

That is why Phase 8's bottom of funnel assets are terms, refund policy, and an
honest exclusion page rather than more persuasion, and why Phase 9 treats billing
shape as a conversion instrument.

## Step 5.2. Design partner profile, the founding cohort

Promoted per `SKILL-ADAPTATION.md`. For a pre-launch community this is the most
valuable step in the phase, because a founding cohort is the only thing that makes
stage 5 of the funnel work in month one.

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| Design partner attributes | Currently holds at least one live or imminent deal with a United States or Canada buyer. Willing to let that deal be discussed in front of others. Will post before being asked. Tolerant of a room that is not yet full and a curriculum that is not yet finished. Responds within 48 hours |
| Job titles | AI consultant, AI service provider, senior or staff engineer with side client work, technical founder pre-revenue in target market, solo agency owner of two to five people |
| Demographics | ICP Level 2 rather than Level 1. Based in the five named markets, weighted to Saudi Arabia and the UAE for rail reasons. Operates in English on client calls, or is close to it |
| Responsibility areas | Owns their own pipeline end to end. No employer between them and the buyer |
| Current tools | Upwork or Mostaql, LinkedIn, email, Payoneer or Wise, a proposal document they wrote themselves and have never had reviewed |
| Key pain points | Won on capability and lost on presentation. Cannot repeat the one target market deal that worked. Priced the first one too low and is stuck there. Nobody around them has done this |
| Critical triggers | Just lost a deal they expected to win. Just landed one and cannot find a second. Just tried to raise a rate and was refused |
| Goals and motivations | Make the second target market deal happen deliberately. Be in a room with people running the same motion. Get their actual proposal looked at by someone who knows the buyer |
| Decision criteria | Is the founder credible. Is anyone else here real. Will my specific deal get attention. Can I pay. Can I leave |
| Influence level | High and disproportionate. These are the people whose posts make the room feel alive to everyone who joins in month two. They are also the only available source of proof |
| Barriers to purchase | Nothing to evaluate yet, no site, no testimonials, no track record. Payment rails. Justified scepticism of the category. Fear of an empty room, which is exactly what a founding cohort is |
| Information sources | Arabic technical communities, LinkedIn, X, YouTube, WhatsApp and Discord groups, direct referral from someone they trust |
| Success metrics | They post in the first week without prompting. They bring a real deal to a teardown. They are still active at day 30. They say something out loud about the experience that can be quoted with permission |
| Early adopter characteristics | Has paid for a course or community before and has opinions about why it failed. Comfortable being visibly mid-process. Will tell the founder directly when something is not working, which is the single most valuable behaviour available at this stage |

### How many, and how to get them

**Target: 8 to 12.** Below 8 the room is visibly empty and every member can see it.
Above 12 the founder cannot give each one real attention, and personal attention is
the entire reason a founding cohort works.

**Recruit, do not accept.** Direct outreach to individuals who match the profile,
one at a time, by the founder. Not a waitlist, not an open launch. The research says
30 to 50 percent of a badly onboarded cohort churns inside 14 days, and an open
launch is an onboarding process with no selection in it.

**What to offer them.** Access at a price that is honest about what does not exist
yet, and permanent terms as the group that took the risk. Not a discount framed as a
favour, an exchange: a lower price and direct founder access, for feedback, for
being visible in the room, and for permission to reference their experience later.

**The founder credential problem is at its sharpest here.** These 8 to 12 people are
being asked to commit before there is anything to evaluate. `00-ASSUMPTIONS.md`
decision 5 is not an empty field in a template at this point, it is the entire
pitch. Until the founder can say what they have actually done, founding cohort
outreach is a conversation with no evidence in it.
