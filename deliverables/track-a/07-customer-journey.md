## Key journey insights

### Critical success factors

1. **The sales leader is present before the proposal.** Phase 5 explains the failure
   mode in full. Stage 3 has this as a binary KPI because it is the one variable that
   most strongly predicts whether the engagement produces anything.
2. **The diagnostic is real work, given away.** It is what substitutes for a track
   record at stage 3. A diagnostic that is a sales call with a document attached
   fails at exactly the moment credibility is being tested.
3. **Sessions run on live pipeline.** Reps discount anything that does not touch
   their number, and stage 5 fails quietly when they do.
4. **Reference permission is signed at stage 4.** Not requested at stage 6. This is
   the difference between one engagement and a track record.

### Primary journey blockers

1. **No track record, and stage 3 is where that lands.** Phase 2 scored credibility
   at 1.8. Every prospect will ask who else this has been done for and the honest
   answer is nobody yet. The diagnostic and an institutional partnership are the two
   available substitutes and neither fully replaces a reference.
2. **The sales leader kills it quietly.** Not by objecting. By scheduling. Phase 5
   has the five practices that prevent it. Treat a founder's refusal to include the
   sales leader as a disqualification rather than an obstacle.
3. **Cross border contracting and payment.** The Track A version of the payment
   blocker, and different from Track B's. Not a card limit, but the friction of a
   foreign vendor engagement: procurement, foreign supplier registration, currency
   of invoice, withholding tax treatment, and whether payment needs central bank
   approval in the client's market. A company in a currency-controlled market may
   need approval to pay a foreign invoice at all. Establish the payment route during
   stage 4, not after signature, and price the delay into the cash flow plan.
4. **The board prefers a hire.** A hire looks like ownership and appears on an
   org chart. Phase 9 anchor 2 is built specifically to answer this and it is the
   strongest artifact available for this buyer.

### Added from Block 1 research, what stage 1 actually is

Track A's stage 1 trigger is a founder saying target market deals close unpredictably
and stall after the pitch. `00-BUYER-CHECKS.md` says where.

A North American buyer runs intake, risk tiering, due diligence, contracting,
operational setup and go-live, across four lanes that must all clear. Above roughly
$25,000 annual contract value, procurement, legal review and a security questionnaire
become normal. Above $100,000 the cycle runs 90 to 180 plus days.

The four gaps most commonly missing from a foreign vendor's setup are mature security
evidence, an accurate W-8BEN or W-8BEN-E, documented insurance at expected limits,
and attributable proof assets. Three of those four are invisible to a sales team
that has only ever sold at home, because the home market never asked.

**Consequence for this funnel.** The diagnostic at stage 3 should test the client
against these gates directly. It turns a diagnostic that currently rests on the
founder's judgement into one that rests on a published checklist, which matters a
great deal for a business Phase 2 scored at 1.8 on credibility.

### Acceleration opportunities

1. **Partnerships compress stages 1 to 3.** A trade body or export programme
   introduction arrives with borrowed credibility attached, which is exactly the
   thing missing at stage 3. This is why Phase 8 treats partnerships as the priority
   channel rather than one of three.
2. **Vertical concentration compresses stage 2 and stage 5.** The second client in a
   vertical gets a sharper diagnostic in less time, per Phase 1.
3. **Give the diagnostic away properly.** Half a diagnostic is worse than none. It
   demonstrates exactly the caution that a buyer with no reference to check is
   watching for.
4. **Sell the second engagement during stage 6, not after stage 7.** A client who
   has just seen a deal progress further than before is at the highest point of
   perceived value in the entire journey.
