## Step 6.1. Brand promise and positioning

| Element | Definition |
|---|---|
| Brand promise | Your existing sales team will know what a buyer in the United States or Canada checks about a foreign vendor, and you will hold the strategy and the capability after the engagement ends |
| Positioning | One route, Arab technology companies into the United States and Canada, with the capability transferred into the client's own team rather than rented or hired |

## Step 6.2, Table 1. Pillars

One pillar per persona, because Phase 5 found three genuinely different people
blocking for three genuinely different reasons.

| Value prop | Description | RTB 1 | RTB 2 | RTB 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **VP1. What this buyer checks** *(the founder)* | Destination specific knowledge that no general sales methodology carries and no one inside the company currently has | RTB 1.1 Market entry readiness diagnostic, delivered before the engagement is sold | RTB 1.2 Target market buyer research and ICP definition, by vertical | RTB 1.3 Pricing reviewed against target market comparables rather than local ones |
| **VP2. It stays in your team** *(the sales leader)* | The alternative to a hire whose knowledge leaves with them and an agency that never had it inside the company | RTB 2.1 Training delivered to the existing team, co-scheduled and co-authored with the sales leader | RTB 2.2 A named handover artifact at the end of every engagement | RTB 2.3 Sales assets and messaging rebuilt with the team, not for them |
| **VP3. Deliberate, not accidental** *(the rep, and the forecast)* | Target market conversations are already happening unmanaged. This makes them repeatable and forecastable | RTB 3.1 Work applied to the client's live target market deals during the engagement | RTB 3.2 Strategy and training bundled, so the plan has executors and the training has a direction | RTB 3.3 Advisory retainer available after handover, and not before |
