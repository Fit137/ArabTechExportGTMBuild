## Step 5.1, ICP Level 2. Early traction, no strategy or trained team

Same three roles, shifted by the fact that target market deals are already arriving.

| Attribute | User, the rep | Manager, VP Sales | Decision maker, founder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Possible job titles | As above, plus whoever informally handles foreign inbound | As above. May already own an unofficial target market motion | As above |
| Purchasing influence | Low on decision, decisive on outcome | Highest practical influence, and now with evidence of the problem | Holds budget, and now under pressure to forecast |
| **Potential reasons to block** | We already closed some, so what is missing. I am the one who closed them, so this implies I did it wrong | Softer than Level 1. The evidence is on the table already. The remaining objection is capacity and sequencing, not premise | Why pay for this when it is already happening. Answer: it is happening and it is not forecastable, which is the actual problem |
| Drivers | Stop losing at procurement and security review after winning the pitch | Turn scattered wins into a forecast. Own the motion officially | Justify a target market hire or entity with evidence rather than hope |
| Assets to get buy-in | A teardown of one of their own stalled deals | Analysis of their existing target market wins, showing what was accidental in them | Evidence that the current motion is unrepeatable, drawn from their own pipeline |

**The Level 2 sale is materially easier** and it is why Phase 4 recommends winning
it first. The premise is already accepted. The conversation is about sequencing.

## The manager problem, and how to avoid it

`SKILL-ADAPTATION.md` singles this out and it deserves the space.

A founder buys sales training. The sales leader hears that their team has been
judged insufficient by someone who did not consult them. What follows is not
refusal. It is scheduling difficulty, partial attendance, no follow-through, and a
measured explanation afterwards of why the content did not fit this team. The
engagement fails and nothing in the content caused it.

| Rule | Practice |
|---|---|
| The manager is in the room before the proposal | The diagnostic is delivered to the founder and the sales leader together, or to the sales leader first. Never to the founder alone |
| The gap is destination, never competence | Nobody in the company has sold to this buyer, including people who are excellent at selling. That framing is both true and the only one a sales leader can accept publicly |
| The manager co-authors the plan | Their name on the sequencing, their call on which reps and when. Ownership is what converts a judgement into a project |
| Scheduling is agreed against their quarter | Their capacity objection is usually genuine. Treat it as a real constraint rather than as resistance |
| Success is defined as the manager's win | The engagement's stated outcome is a sales leader who can forecast target market revenue. Not a team that was fixed |

If the founder will not put the sales leader in the room before the proposal, that is
a qualification signal, not an obstacle to work around. The engagement will fail and
it will be recorded as the vendor's failure.

## Step 5.2. First client profile

Track B recruits a founding cohort. Track A needs a first client, and the same logic
applies: it is selected, not accepted.

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| Design partner attributes | ICP Level 2. Already has target market deals arriving unpredictably. Sales leader is willing and available. Founder will give the engagement real attention. Willing to be referenced later |
| Job titles | Founder or CEO initiating, with a VP Sales or Sales Director who is a genuine participant |
| Demographics | 10 to 60 people. Established product. Sales function of at least three. In one of the named markets |
| Current tools | A CRM in partial use, sales assets written for the home market, pricing anchored to local comparables, at least one target market deal in the pipeline right now |
| Key pain points | Target market deals stall after the pitch, at procurement, security review, or contracting. Wins cannot be explained or repeated |
| Critical triggers | A stalled deal they can name. A rep failing abroad who succeeds at home. A board question about international revenue |
| Goals and motivations | Make the motion repeatable and forecastable before committing to a hire or an entity |
| Decision criteria | Does this person understand our buyer. Will our sales leader accept it. What do we hold at the end. What does it cost against the alternatives |
| Influence level | Very high. The first Track A client is the entire track record. Everything in Phase 2 scored 1.8 depends on this engagement going well |
| Barriers to purchase | No track record, no references, no case study. The single largest barrier and it is unavoidable |
| Information sources | Peer founders, trade bodies and export programmes, accelerator networks, LinkedIn |
| Success metrics | The sales leader stays engaged to the end. A named handover artifact exists. They agree to be referenced. They buy a second thing, retainer or second market |
| Early adopter characteristics | Has bought consulting before and knows what a bad engagement looks like. Will say so directly. Values a practitioner over a brand |

**Price the first engagement for the reference, not for the fee.** A completed
engagement with permission to be named moves the Phase 2 credibility score from 1.8
to something a second buyer will accept, and nothing else available does that.

**One condition to hold.** Permission to be referenced is agreed in writing at the
start, not asked for at the end. Asking afterwards produces a polite decline from a
client who is otherwise happy, and Phase 6 stays blocked for another six months.
