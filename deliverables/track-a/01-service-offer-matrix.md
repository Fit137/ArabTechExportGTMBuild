## Step 1.4. Quadrant assignment

| Service item | Quadrant | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Market entry strategy | 1. Hero | Highest demand in the set and genuinely differentiated. It is what the buyer names when they describe the problem, and the research found no named specialist running this direction |
| Sales asset and messaging rebuild | 2. Differentiator | Highest differentiation after pricing. Demand is lower because the buyer does not believe their assets are the problem until a target market buyer ignores them |
| Pricing review against target market comparables | 2. Differentiator | The highest differentiation score in the set and the lowest demand of the paid items. Companies do not ask for this. It is also the single item closest to the core thesis |
| Buyer research and ICP definition | 2. Differentiator | Real and defensible, usually bought as part of market entry rather than alone |
| Sales team training programme | 4. Table stakes | High demand, negative differentiation. Sandler and Winning by Design both sell this credibly and at scale. It is the entry point, not the reason to choose |
| Ongoing advisory retainer | 3. Commodity | The only negative demand score. Nobody buys a retainer before the first engagement proves anything. It is a renewal product, not an acquisition one |

**The tension to manage.** Item 1 is what the client asks for and item 2 is what
they need. Selling the strategy without the training produces a document nobody
executes. Selling the training without the strategy trains a team to sell the wrong
thing in the wrong market. The engagement should bundle them, with item 1 as the
visible line and item 2 as the thing that makes it work.

| 4 | Sales asset and messaging rebuild | Medium to high | Bundled or upsell | Mid | Differentiator |

| 6 | Advisory retainer | Low ongoing | Renewal only | Recurring | Renewal |

### The finding

Track A has the opposite problem to Track B. There is no scaling issue because
there is no scale, every item is founder delivered and priced accordingly. The
problem is that items 2 and 3, the two highest value items, are also the two most
research heavy, and research for a first client in a new sub-sector cannot be
reused.

**What that means commercially.** The first two or three Track A engagements are
loss-making in hours even if they are profitable in fees. Sector concentration is
the answer: three clients in one vertical make the third engagement dramatically
cheaper to deliver than the first. Take the second client from the same vertical as
the first, even at a lower fee.

**What that means for run order.** `GTM-BRIEF.md` recommends Track B first, and
this finding supports it. Track A cannot be run at volume by one person, and its
unit economics improve only with vertical repetition, which takes time this
business has not spent yet.

## Step 1.6. Demand driver

| Attribute | Description |
|---|---|
| What it is | A market entry readiness assessment scoring a company on the six things that decide whether a target market motion will work, delivered as a short written diagnostic rather than a score alone |
| Immediate value | A named list of what is missing, in a form a founder can put in front of their own board or their own sales leader |
| How it helps in problem identification | It separates what the company thinks is blocking them, usually leads, from what actually is, usually that the offer is described for a buyer who already knows them |
| How it exposes the need | A gap in strategy and positioning cannot be fixed by more outbound activity, which is what this buyer does next by default |
| ICP fit | Direct. ICP Level 1 in Phase 4, established at home, no deliberate target market motion |
| Conversion pull | The diagnostic is the first deliverable of the engagement, produced before the engagement is sold. It demonstrates the method rather than describing it |
| Top of funnel use case | Secondary. Enterprise buyers do not self serve into a scorecard. This runs as the opening of a conversation, not as an ad |

*Field renamed from the skill's "Leverage for conversion" per the word list in `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5. Same field, same position in the skill's Step 1.6 table.*

**Note on the difference from Track B.** Track B's driver is a self serve scorecard
that scales infinitely. Track A's is a diagnostic that costs founder hours per
prospect. That is correct for the ticket size, and it caps how many Track A
conversations can run at once. Roughly four to six live at a time for one person.

## Added from Block 1 research, a seventh service item to rate

`00-BUYER-CHECKS.md` validates Track A more strongly than anything else in the run.
Track A's clients sell at deal sizes where every gate in that research is real:
procurement, legal review and a security questionnaire become normal above roughly
$25,000 annual contract value, and above $100,000 the cycle runs 90 to 180 plus days
with a reported 35 to 40 percent of elapsed time inside legal and procurement.

That surfaces a service item the original list did not have.

| # | Service item | Demand | Competitiveness | Delivery effort | Funnel role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | **Vendor onboarding readiness.** Getting the client through a North American buyer's procurement, security, legal and finance lanes | Wanted by most, once they have stalled once | Unique to us | High once, then partly reusable across clients in a vertical | **Differentiator, and the most concrete deliverable in the set** |

Scored provisionally at demand 11.2, competitiveness 16.4, which places it in
quadrant 1 alongside market entry strategy.

**Why it belongs.** Phase 7 stage 1 already records that Track A's buyer describes
the problem as deals stalling after the pitch. The research names exactly where they
stall and what clears it: W-8BEN-E accuracy, a security evidence pack in place of
SOC 2, insurance certificates, a DPA, and proof assets the client's champion can
forward to four departments. That is a scopeable, checkable deliverable, which is
rare in this offer set.

**Why it also improves the economics.** Phase 1 found the first two or three
engagements are loss-making in hours because buyer research does not transfer across
sub-sectors. Vendor onboarding readiness is the exception. The tax forms, the
security pack structure, the contract artifacts and the insurance requirements are
substantially the same for every client. It is the first genuinely reusable asset in
Track A, and it argues for building it before the second engagement rather than
after the fifth.

**Before pricing it, settle `00-CONFLICTS-TO-RESOLVE.md` conflict 6.** Whether a
client in the five origin markets can obtain insurance a North American buyer accepts
is unresearched, and it is part of this deliverable.
