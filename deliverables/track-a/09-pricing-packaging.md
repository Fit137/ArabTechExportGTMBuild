## Step 9.1. Value metric selection

| Denominator | Correlation to value | Scalability | Ease of measurement | Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Per engagement, fixed scope | High. Matches how the buyer budgets and how procurement approves | Low. Capped by founder hours | Trivial | **8.9** |
| Per market entered | High. The client thinks in markets, one engagement per destination | Medium. Naturally produces a second engagement | Easy | **8.4** |
| Per seat trained | Medium. Correlates with delivery cost, not with value | Medium | Easy | **5.2** |
| Per month, retainer | Low pre-handover, high post-handover | High | Trivial | **6.7**, post-handover only |
| Per outcome, revenue share or success fee | **Excluded, not scored** | | | **Dropped** |

**Why per outcome is excluded.** Same reason as Track B and one more. It is an
outcome claim expressed as a payment schedule, it is banned by `GTM-BRIEF.md`
Section 5, and for Track A it also creates an attribution argument the business
cannot win. Market entry revenue has many parents. A success fee turns every
invoice into a negotiation about causation.

**Recommendation**

- **Primary: per engagement, fixed scope, fixed duration.** Procurement can approve
  it and the founder can plan capacity against it.
- **Secondary: per market.** The natural unit of expansion, and it makes a second
  engagement a scope decision rather than a fresh sale.
- **Retainer: monthly, after handover only.** Phase 6 drill 9. A retainer sold before
  handover is a way of never handing over.

### Three rules that matter more than the numbers

1. **The diagnostic is free and it is real work.** It substitutes for the track
   record this business does not have, per Phase 7 stage 3. Charging for it converts
   worse and, more importantly, a diagnostic hedged to protect billable scope
   demonstrates exactly the caution a buyer with no reference is watching for.

2. **The second market price is quoted with the first.** It makes the first
   engagement a beginning rather than a transaction, it is honest about the real
   economics, since Phase 1 showed the second is genuinely cheaper to deliver, and it
   gives the founder a forecast.

3. **The retainer is unavailable until handover is delivered and used.** Stated as a
   rule in the proposal. It is a strong trust signal precisely because it costs the
   business revenue to hold.

### Contracting terms to settle before signature

Phase 7 stage 4 blocker, in the proposal rather than discovered afterwards.

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| Currency of invoice | Client's local currency or USD. It changes who carries the exchange risk |
| Foreign supplier registration | Some clients need the vendor registered before any invoice can be paid |
| Withholding tax treatment | Can reduce received fees materially and is almost never raised by the client until the first payment |
| Central bank or regulatory approval | Required in some currency-controlled markets before a foreign invoice can be paid at all |
| Payment schedule against milestones | Protects cash flow through a 10 to 16 week engagement |
| **Reference permission** | **In writing, at the start.** Phase 5 and Phase 6. Unblocks eighteen fields in Phase 6 and moves Phase 2's credibility score |

### Anchor 2. Alternative comparison. The one that closes

| Alternative | Published cost | What it leaves behind |
|---|---|---|
| **Hire a target market sales leader** | Median SaaS VP Sales base about $157,500, typical range $115,000 to $185,000. Series B on target earnings $360,000 to $425,000 at a 60/40 split. Average time to fill about 106 days, 10 to 16 weeks with a dedicated recruiter, over 20 weeks for AI specific searches. Three to six months ramp. Executive search fees on top | Relationships and knowledge that leave when they do. Concentrated risk on one person, before ramp is complete |
| **Outsourced sales agency** | Retainer plus commission, ongoing for as long as it runs | Nothing inside the company. The relationship and the learning stay with the agency |
| **A Big Four or MBB engagement** | $400 to $800 per hour for Big Four, $500 to $1,000 plus for MBB partners | Analysis. Execution inside the client's own sales team is not part of it |
| **Do nothing differently** | No invoice | The unmanaged motion continues, unforecastable, per Phase 3 statement 3 |
| **This engagement** | `{TBD}` | Playbook, buyer research, rebuilt assets, a trained team, and a named handover artifact |

**How to use this, and how not to.** Set the engagement fee against the hire's first
year, including ramp and search fees, and against the fact that roughly 106 days pass
before the hire starts and another three to six months before they are productive.
That comparison is factual, sourced, and it answers the board's instinct directly.

Do not extend it into a claim that the engagement produces the revenue a sales leader
would have produced. That is an outcome claim and it is banned.

### The calculator, deliberately absent

The skill's "one outcome pays for X years" calculator does not appear. For Track A it
would read as "one closed deal pays for the engagement", which is an outcome claim
with arithmetic attached and an attribution argument waiting inside it. Dropped, and
not to be reintroduced in any later asset.
