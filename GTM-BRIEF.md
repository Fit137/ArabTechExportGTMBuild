The filled discovery input for the `gtm-builder` skill, v3.1. This is what the skill's REQUIRED INPUTS checklist asks for, answered.

Read `SKILL-ADAPTATION.md` before Phase 1. The skill is written for B2B SaaS and this is a services and education business, so six of the nine phases need a documented substitution.

**Global rule for the whole run, taken from the skill's own deck prompt and applied everywhere:** if a metric is unknown, keep it as `{TBD}` and do not invent numbers. Arab Tech Export is pre-launch. It has no customers, no revenue, no results, and no testimonials, so every proof point, outcome, count, and benchmark is `{TBD}` until the founder supplies it.

## Run order

The business has two offers for two different buyers. The skill builds one GTM per run, so it runs twice.

**Run 1: Track B, individual builders.** Do this first. The offer is closest to a product, the competitor set is researchable, and the skill's Phase 9 subscription tiers fit it natively.

**Run 2: Track A, enterprise.** Separate run, separate competitor slate, separate pricing logic. Phase 9 switches from subscription tiers to engagement pricing.

Everything below is given per track. Where a field is shared, it says so.

# 2. COMPETITORS

The skill wants 5 to 6 named competitors with websites, and its Step 2.1 is an external research step where you source them. Nothing below names a company, because no research has been done and a fabricated competitor would poison Phase 2 and every phase that reads from it.

What the brief gives you instead is the slate: which category each of the six slots comes from, and the rule for filling it. `RESEARCH-PROMPTS.md` holds the ready-to-paste research prompt.

**Selection rule:** a competitor earns a slot only if your ICP actually evaluates it. Not who you think of as a rival, who they consider instead. In both tracks the most common answer is doing nothing, and the second is a category that is not education at all.

## Run 1, Track B slate

| Slot | Source from | Why it belongs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A paid community or cohort course teaching technical people to sell to Western clients | The closest direct substitute |
| 2 | A large free-content creator in the same space | The real competition. Enormous supply, zero cost, often good |

| 4 | A general freelancing or consulting business course | Adjacent, better known, and what a buyer finds first when searching |
| 5 | A regional accelerator, incubator, or founder program serving the same origin markets | Structured, credible, often subsidized |
| 6 | A government or trade export program in an origin market in scope | Usually a partner rather than a rival, and worth mapping as both |

# 5. POSITIONING AND CLAIM CONSTRAINTS

Phase 3 writes positioning statements, Phase 6 writes 27 headlines across nine copy drills, and Phase 10 writes the deck. All three will reach for the thesis. Four claims in it need a narrowed form, because the broad form is either unprovable or lands badly on the audience being sold to.

Copy about Arab builders is read by Arab builders. Compare economics, never readiness.

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| The endpoint of an AI service is replacing humans | An AI system is priced against the cost of the work it addresses. Where that work is expensive to staff, the same system addresses a more expensive problem |
| Local markets are not ready for AI, or are resistant to it | Buyer economics and adoption timing differ between markets |
| Premium prices and exponentially higher revenue | The same capability can be priced differently depending on what the buyer compares it against. What a given builder captures depends on their offer, positioning, and the deals they win |
| The only provider pairing this origin with these markets | Describe what the business is built around, a specific origin and a specific destination. No superlative until the Phase 2 research supports one |

Also banned in every phase: any student or client count, any income or revenue figure achieved by anyone, any testimonial, any named logo, any market size or salary comparison or adoption rate, any employment, placement, or visa outcome, and any guarantee of a result.

Mechanics, matching the skill's own deck rule: no em dashes. Also avoid leverage, unlock, seamless, robust, streamline, cutting-edge, and empower.
